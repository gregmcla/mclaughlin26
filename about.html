About

I keep ending up as one of the first commercial people on something technical that does not have a playbook yet. Lately I have been building the playbook too.

I studied risk management and insurance at Temple and went straight into Life.io, an early-stage insurtech, as one of the first ten employees. That is where I learned that in a new category you have to create the credibility before you can sell anything. I wrote three white papers, grew a research newsletter past 400 subscribers, and we won 25 customers before the company was acquired.

At Curalate I managed enterprise brands through adoption, expansion, and renewal, and learned that the sale is not the outcome. Usage is. At HGS Digital I built the channel and partner-enablement function for a new cloud business from nothing: joint go-to-market with AWS, Twilio, and Sprinklr, the co-sell narratives, the playbooks, and the enablement that gets another company's rep to carry your product into a meeting.

At Laskie I was employee 6 and the first business development hire at a two-sided recruiting marketplace. I built outbound from zero on both sides, launched partnerships into a global network of 20+ recruiting firms plus a program that paid independent recruiters directly, worked with engineering to architect the platform partners used to submit and track candidates, and hired and managed three account executives and an operations manager. The company went from pre-revenue to about $2.5M in its first year of revenue and was acquired by X. At six people everyone touches everything, so I am careful with that number. What was mine was both outbound motions, the partner network, the program economics, and the team.

At Kharon, sanctions and risk intelligence for major financial institutions, I managed about 12 strategic partners across enterprise data, technology, and advisory channels. Most were signed and few were producing, which is the normal state of a partner program, so the work was concentrating on the few where someone on the other side had a commercial reason to care.

Then two years inside a sell-side M&A firm in Tampa, building and running a network of 100+ attorneys, CPAs, wealth advisors, and bankers who refer business owners when they are ready to sell. I was in the room every day with the people most AI companies are now trying to reach and have not figured out how to sell to. And because the workflows were painful, I started building. That is where the record comes from.

On the technical question

I am not an engineer. I build with AI coding tools, design agent workflows with deterministic checks and human approval steps, and spend real time on evaluation and failure modes. Enough to be useful in a room with your engineering team rather than decorative, and enough to know where these systems break, because I have watched mine break and written it down.
