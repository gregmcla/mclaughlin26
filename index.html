How I work

When a company asks me about putting AI into a workflow, these are the four questions I ask first.

Where does the judgment happen?

The model handles the ambiguous, unstructured step. That is what it is good at, and boxing it in up front makes it worse without making it safer.

Where are the hard rules?

Plain code enforces anything that has to be right every time. The model can usually get it right. "Usually" is not a standard a business can run on.

Where does the person sit?

A person owns every decision that matters. The system's job is to make that review fast enough not to slow things down and structured enough not to become rubber-stamping.

How will you know in ninety days?

Every decision is logged with its reasoning, somewhere the model cannot edit. Without that record, nobody can answer "is this working?" and someone always asks.

Since then I have been one of the first commercial people in the room at six technology and advisory companies, three of which were acquired while I was there. At Laskie I was employee 6, built outbound on both sides of a two-sided marketplace, grew a partner network of 20+ recruiting firms, and managed a team of four.

I am not a software engineer. I design the system, write the spec, direct AI coding agents, and test what they build against how it is supposed to behave. Everything that isn't the model is the part I know how to build.
